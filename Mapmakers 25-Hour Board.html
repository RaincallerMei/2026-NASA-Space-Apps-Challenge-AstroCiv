Big pictureThis is not a resource game with a story on top. It is a story about a dream of exploring space. Managing power, air, water and food is how the player lives that dream.

Every great civilization starts with a dream.

A child who loved building civilizations on a computer gets to build humanity's first real lunar settlement.

Three ideas

Wonder

What does it feel like to look at the Moon and imagine living there?

Felt in: bedroom, the drawing, the sunrise.

Responsibility

What does it take to keep people alive in an unfamiliar world?

Felt in: power, air, water, food, crew chat, the storm.

Possibility

Could a kid from Mississauga become part of humanity's space story?

Felt in: Hadfield and Seager moment, photo cards, final message.

Honesty rules for the story

  • Seager and Hadfield: names as inspiration only. No faces, no quotes, no hint they back the game. Check every fact on their own pages.
  • Seager was TESS Deputy Science Director. Not the director.
  • Say once: the astronaut path is a dream, not a promise.
  • Science event, teacher and crew are made up. Say so.
  • Write "Earth hangs low above the horizon". Do not write "Earthrise". From most Moon spots Earth barely moves.
  • Storm and sunrise are separate. A solar warning never turns panels off.
  • Game numbers (kW, kWh, costs) are made-up balance numbers. Label them.
  • Story text under about 650 words. Lines up to 14 words.

Make Midjourney look like real pixel art

  • Ask for: "16-bit pixel art, top-down, limited palette, flat colours".
  • Shrink to 480 × 270 with nearest-neighbour. Scenes then scale ×4 to 1080p.
  • Crew portraits: ask for a face, then shrink to 32 × 32 and clean by hand.
  • Cut colours to the same 16 to 32 as the tile pack.

Submission procedure

  1. Confirm everyone is accepted at the same event. Verify its deadline and time zone with your Local Lead.
  2. Space Apps profile icon → My Team → Project → Edit. Use the edit lock while one person works; teammates can override it.
  3. Complete every required field. Click Save Progress often. Saving alone does not submit the project.
  4. Upload JPG/PNG/GIF images up to 10 MB. Host videos/documents externally; no direct video/document uploads. All submitted text and linked materials must be English, with English subtitles for non-English speech.
  5. Test both demo and final project links without login, password, registration or permission requests. Start a mission from the public game. Review the public-access record; ask an outside visitor to repeat the check.
  6. Review source/asset permissions and originality. The guide requires generated image/video watermarks, and descriptive-text plus metadata acknowledgement for generated audio/code/data. Generated content must not contain NASA branding/design elements.
  7. Review the guide's under-18 likeness clause against the final project and demo. Do not record child testers or use their voices/likenesses. The team must resolve how the Terms & Conditions apply to fictional child art; this draft makes no eligibility determination.
  8. A team member reviews and checks Terms and Conditions and Confirm All Work is Original, then clicks Submit for Judging before the deadline.
  9. Verify the confirmation banner/message and save evidence. If editing again before the deadline, save/resubmit as needed and verify confirmation again. Only the last saved submission is reviewed.

Optional: request FortiGuard classification if site filtering blocks judges. After submission, download the participant certificate through Profile → Participant Information; use a desktop and Letter paper size.
