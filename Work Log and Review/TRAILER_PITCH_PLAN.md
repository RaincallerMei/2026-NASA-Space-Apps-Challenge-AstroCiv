# AstroCiv: proposed 29-second judging trailer

Prepared November 15, 2026. **Draft for the team's review.** This document proposes the pitch, shot order and ending. Producing the revised trailer or ending follows the user's review.

**The central promise:** AstroCiv turns children's curiosity about space into decisions about keeping people alive.

**Why this project exists:** The challenge identifies a gap between space enthusiasm and understanding the engineering decisions that sustain a mission. AstroCiv gives children aged 7-12 a place to experiment with connected systems, observe the cost of their decisions, and try another strategy. The immediate educational goal is understanding a constraint and its consequences. Future interest in exploration is an intended benefit; learning outcomes have not yet been measured.

**Why a game fits:** A player can spend power on food, watch reserves change, reconsider priorities and try again. The interactive feedback makes the trade-off tangible. The story gives those decisions a human purpose: a crew needs the base the player is building.

**Audience:** Children aged 7-12 are the players. Parents, teachers and libraries are possible routes to those players. Judges need clear evidence of challenge alignment, scientific grounding, a working product and its intended educational value.

## How to make the two central mechanics understandable

**Resource shot, 3-11 seconds:**

1. About 3-4.5: hold on battery/power while the simulated mission runs. Establish the problem with an actual changing rate or reserve.
2. About 4.5-6.5: cut to the selected Greenhouse and the real Turn off button. Keep the pointer and click visible.
3. About 6.5-9: hold on the updated power and food rates. Let viewers see both sides of the decision.
4. About 9-11: return to the base with the Greenhouse visibly off and Air Support operating. Keep the same short caption to give the meaning time to land.

Use a setup in which the Greenhouse is initially powered and the power improvement is visible. Switching off an already unpowered building would not demonstrate the intended resource choice. The water cost can remain visible in the interface; power and food should carry the main explanation. The later Shelter activation demonstrates switching a building on.

**Placement shot, 11-16 seconds:**

Use two real takes from equivalent states, or a genuine remove-and-rebuild sequence if it fits. Label a comparison clearly. The game has no verified drag-to-relocate feature. Show one comparison clearly rather than rushing through several building types. The Moon sunlight comparison is preferred because it demonstrates how a NASA dataset affects the player's choice.

The user's distance examples are also implemented and could replace the sunlight comparison:

- A Solar Array beyond the configured Battery connection range loses **30% of its output**. It continues generating power. Keep sunlight equal when comparing distance.
- Air Support beyond its short-duct range produces **half the normal air refill**. Keep other conditions equal when comparing it with a nearby placement.

These distance penalties are game rules, not NASA measurements. For the data-driven sunlight example, use the regular NASA-derived Moon mission map. Free Build and the training mission use generated practice terrain.

## Proposed ending for approval

**Recommended visual concept:** An adult commander stands beside the observation window, Earth visible beyond the base. A communication screen carries Moon-base drawings. The camera settles on a drawing that resembles the successful base. That connects imagining, playing and sharing what was learned.

**Recommended final line:** **“Tomorrow's explorers start here.”**

The line works because the preceding gameplay has shown what “start” means: making a choice, seeing its cost and improving a plan. The title card then gives the viewer a way to act: **“Play AstroCiv in your browser.”**

The existing ending image has illustrated children on its communication screen. The supplied submission guide, p. 13, explicitly prohibits under-18 likenesses in demonstrations and final projects. It does not state an exception for fictional or generated illustrations, and the linked legal page was unavailable. That exception cannot be assumed. The proposed submission visual uses adult crew and drawings; the existing classroom imagery remains a review issue for the trailer and game. No art changes are made by this plan.

**Optional spoken script, aligned with the seven beats:**

> Could you keep a Moon crew alive? Save power. Grow less food. Every choice has a cost. Build where NASA's maps show more sunlight. Solar storm warning. Bring your crew to safety. Live your astronaut story. Experiment in Free Build. Tomorrow's explorers start here. Play AstroCiv.

This is a proposed narration only. Captions and gameplay can carry the pitch without speech. If narration is used, time the recorded delivery against the actual edit.

## Positioning and factual limits

**The project's distinctive combination:** a personal astronaut story, connected resource decisions, spatial planning, NASA-derived terrain and a repeatable mission experiment designed for ages 7-12. The strongest innovation claim is that the scientific information has consequences inside play.

**Artemis context:** NASA's [current Artemis II page](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/artemis-ii/) identifies the mission as a crewed lunar flyby, launched April 1 and returned April 10, 2026. For this November 2026 pitch, a suitable background sentence is: “As human exploration moves toward a sustained lunar presence, children need a way to understand what living beyond Earth requires.” Keep this fuller explanation in the submission; the short hook can begin directly with the player's mission.

**Coverage gap:** The challenge's objectives explicitly include communication delay. The reviewed implementation has a Radio Dish, crew conversations and recall actions; a light-travel communication-delay mechanic was not found. Describe these features accurately and list delay as an unimplemented objective in the written submission. The trailer cannot establish complete coverage of every named system.

**Replay wording:** Free Build has randomized maps/events and a finite mission duration. Use “replayable” or “Free Build.” “Infinite mode” would misdescribe the current implementation.

**Science wording:** Archived NASA data is valid for this challenge. Identify it as historical. LOLA values are average illumination, not live shadows. DONKI metadata informs the event reference; storm timing and crew effects are simplified. The solar-particle warning does not itself switch off Solar Arrays. The powered Shelter is a gameplay abstraction. Do not imply that every displayed resource rate or safety value is a NASA measurement.

**Impact wording:** Say “designed to help children understand” or state the learning opportunity. No measured child-learning or classroom-impact results are documented. The intended transferable habit is testing how a decision affects a connected system.

## Production decisions after review

- Keep real gameplay action and its result adjacent. A simple zoom or cut between the resource strip, selected building and changed rate will carry the explanation.
- Show the actual interface with large English captions. Use color together with words, numbers or arrows. Hold comparison states long enough to read at normal playback speed.
- Retain required visible generated-art indicators when cropping. Credit the specific NASA sources in context; preserve audio and other required disclosures in the published description/metadata. Use plain source text without adding agency logos to generated visuals.
- The current 28-second trailer's manifest allocates 3.4 seconds to the sunlight fact card and 2.8 seconds to the score report. The proposed cut reallocates those holds to a visible toggle consequence and placement comparison. A score screen is optional if readable action footage already proves the result.
- Existing warning, recall, base and colony recordings may be reusable. Verify or capture the toggle-and-rate change, controlled placement comparison and Free Build proof from the intended submission build. The current manifest alone does not establish those missing shots.
- Include confirmed team attribution and selected challenge identification. The final public video link must work without sign-in. The playable destination is [the current browser game](https://raincallermei.github.io/2026-NASA-Space-Apps-Challenge-AstroCiv/).
- After the team approves the structure and ending, produce the requested ending, assemble the cut, verify the exported duration and readability, and review the resulting video before any publication.
